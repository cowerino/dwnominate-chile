# JCC 2026 paper: authoritative numbers

**This directory is the authoritative source for every number printed in the JCC 2026 paper's
empirical section, and for the six-panel optimizer comparison.** If a value in the paper and a
value here disagree, this directory wins and the paper is wrong.

Frozen 2026-08-29. Immutable: supersede with a new dated directory, do not edit these numbers.

## What produced these

A parametric bootstrap of the 2018-2022 Chilean Chamber (55th legislative period), following the
DW-NOMINATE procedure of Lewis and Poole and Carroll et al.

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| replicates | **B = 999**, 999 discovered, **999 successful** (no failures, none discarded) |
| panel | 12 approximately four-month periods, March 2018 to March 2022 |
| model | two-dimensional, **linear** trajectories (order 1), four matched cycles |
| engine | modern (NLopt: BOBYQA for the scalar blocks, COBYLA for the constrained blocks) |
| phase endpoints | P1 = model unit 5, P2 = unit 8, P3 = unit 12 |
| alignment | `d1-sign`; each replicate aligned to the observed D1 by sign and location |
| confidence | 0.95 |
| starting values | regenerated by re-running W-NOMINATE **inside every replicate** |

Each replicate redraws every observed vote from its fitted probability, retaining the observed
membership, roll-call set and missing-data pattern, then reruns the entire estimator on the
synthetic record.

## Two traps in these files

**1. The "typical formal party" pivot excludes the independents. `observed_global_party_shift.csv`
does not.**

The paper prints a typical formal party change of **-0.153** (full delegation) and **-0.140**
(continuous core). `observed_global_party_shift.csv` prints `-0.15895` and `-0.14278` for the same
contrast. Both are correct; they answer different questions. The global file takes the median over
**all** eligible categories including `IND`, a residual category. The paper takes the median over
eligible **formal parties**, with `IND` excluded and reported as its own row.

**The exclusion is a substantive decision, not a filter.** The independents are not a party: they
are a residual bucket of members who share no label, no whip and no organization. They therefore
also have no meaningful *continuous core*, because "the same deputies, in the same party, across
the whole term" presupposes a party to stay in. A statistic named "the typical formal party" is a
statement about party behaviour, so a bucket that is not a party does not belong in its median.
`IND` is still reported, as its own row, because its movement is a real fact about the chamber.

Reproduce the paper's value from `party_change_intervals.csv`:
filter `target_a=P1, target_b=P3, eligible_party=True`, drop `party=IND`, take the median of
`observed_delta`. Full delegation gives 9 parties, median `-0.1529` -> **-0.153**. Continuous core
gives 8 parties, median of the 4th and 5th gives `-0.1404` -> **-0.140**.

Do not "correct" the paper to -0.159. That would silently fold the independents into a statistic
the paper defines as being about formal parties.

**2. Levels differ by cohort.** `median_a`/`median_b` are cohort-specific. The independents' full
delegation runs -0.554 to -0.776 while its continuous core runs -0.174 to -0.359, because the two
cohorts are different sets of people. Table III prints full-delegation levels and both cohorts'
changes.

## Still unsourced here

The **bootstrap SE of 0.033** on the typical-party pivot is not derivable from the summary files in
this directory; it needs the replicate-level bank of the pivot itself
(`bootstrap_global_party_shift.csv`, ~1 MB, not promoted). Either promote that file or drop the SE
from the paper.

## What is deliberately not here

The full replicate banks (`bootstrap_party_medians.csv`, `bootstrap_party_changes.csv`,
`bootstrap_individual_change_medians.csv`, roughly 40 MB together) stay out of the repository. They
are regenerable from the reproduction package, and everything the paper prints is already
summarized in the files above.
